- Nerfs (lower damage or stats, higher cooldowns or mana costs) to a hero's core abilities make pros pick it less: predict "down". Buffs to core abilities make pros pick it more: predict "up".
- Talent swaps, talent number tweaks, minor numeric tweaks, and quality-of-life or text changes are noise, so don't treat them as real buffs or nerfs. If a patch has only these, judge by prior popularity (see next rule) instead of defaulting to "flat".
- Use prior popularity as the main tiebreaker for small or mixed changes. Heroes in the most-picked third (>10%) usually fall ("down") after almost any patch that trims them, mixes buffs and nerfs, or removes a facet or utility. Regression to the mean is strong at the top. Heroes in the least-picked third (<=4.5%) usually rise ("up") after any non-trivial buff or neutral rework, since they have room to grow. Middle-popularity heroes are split, so follow the net direction of the core ability changes.
- Base attribute or stat reworks matter more than they look. A hero that gets a large base damage jump but a much lower per-level damage gain, or that switches primary attribute, is really getting a late-game nerf. The result is a big pick-rate drop for popular carries and a possible rise for low-pick heroes that gain early power or a better attribute.
- Predict "flat" only when the changes are tiny and the hero is mid or low in popularity. Flat is rare (about 15% of cases), so with any substantive change lean to up or down rather than flat.
