- Nerfs (lower damage or stats, higher cooldowns or mana costs) to a hero's core abilities make pros pick it less: predict "down". Buffs to core abilities make pros pick it more: predict "up".
- Talent swaps, talent number tweaks, minor numeric tweaks, and quality-of-life or text changes are not real buffs or nerfs. **For a low or mid-pick hero (<=8%) with no core-ability nerf, a patch of talent swaps, talent buffs, small secondary-ability tweaks or removed facets leans "up", because these heroes tend to drift upward. Reserve "flat" for patches that are truly only text or QoL, or one lone minor number.**
- Use prior popularity as the main tiebreaker for small or mixed changes. Heroes in the most-picked third (>10%) often fall ("down") after trims, mixed changes, or removed facets, but not when they get real base stat or damage gains: then predict "up" or hold. Heroes in the least-picked third (<=4.5%) rise after a clear buff to core abilities. Middle-popularity heroes are split, so follow the net direction of the core ability changes.
- **The 7.36-style base attribute rework (large "Base Damage increased", lower "Damage gain per level", higher stat gains) is usually a mild buff, whatever the level-30 damage drop.** If level-1 damage rises or stays the same and stat gains rise, predict "up" for low, mid and top heroes. Hold at "flat" only when the stat gains are tiny or an ability is also nerfed. Predict "down" only if level-1 damage falls or is unchanged and the hero is mid or low-pick, or if the same patch also cuts a core ability.
- Removing a facet is not by itself a nerf. Treat it as neutral unless the hero's core ability was also cut.
- Ability numeric buffs only count if they hit the hero's main pick reason (primary damage, stun, core spell). Buffs limited to Aghanim's Shard/Scepter, secondary or situational spells, or accompanied by removed utility (e.g. no longer restores mana, no longer kills illusions, removed ability, cast-range or "affects only self" cuts) are net neutral or negative: don't predict "up" from them. A hero that loses a defining ability or mechanic, or has a mixed patch with a real nerf, leans "down" when it was popular or mid-pick.
- Predict "flat" only when the changes are tiny and the hero is mid or low in popularity. Flat is rare (about 15% of cases), so with any substantive change lean to up or down. A hero with an all-talent patch that reshuffles many talents on a popular hero (>9%) leans "down" rather than "flat".
