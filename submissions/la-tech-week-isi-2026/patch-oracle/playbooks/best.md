- Nerfs (lower damage or stats, higher cooldowns or mana costs) to a hero's core abilities make pros pick it less: predict "down". Buffs to core abilities make pros pick it more: predict "up".
- Talent swaps, talent number tweaks, minor numeric tweaks, and quality-of-life or text changes are noise, so don't treat them as real buffs or nerfs. If a patch has only these, judge by prior popularity (see next rule) instead of defaulting to "flat". Very small changes on a mid or low-popularity hero (a couple of talent tweaks, a lifesteal-rule or text change, one minor number) are often truly "flat".
- Use prior popularity as the main tiebreaker for small or mixed changes. Heroes in the most-picked third (>10%) often fall ("down") after trims, mixed changes, or removed facets, but not when they get real base stat or damage gains: then predict "up" or hold. Heroes in the least-picked third (<=4.5%) rise after a clear buff to core abilities, but don't assume "up" from noise-level changes alone. Middle-popularity heroes are split, so follow the net direction of the core ability changes.
- The 7.36-style base attribute rework (large "Base Damage increased", lower "Damage gain per level", higher stat gains) is NOT a reliable signal. If level-1 damage rises or stays the same and stat gains rise, treat it as a mild buff for mid and top heroes ("up"), or "flat" for low-pick heroes. Predict "down" only if level-1 damage is unchanged and the level-30 drop is large, or the hero is low-pick and switches attribute. Low-pick heroes with a big late-game loss (≥35–50 damage) often fall further, even with a small ability buff, so don't call "up" for them.
- Removing a facet is not by itself a nerf. Treat it as neutral unless the hero's core ability was also cut.
- **Ability numeric buffs only count if they hit the hero's main pick reason (primary damage, stun, core spell).** Buffs limited to Aghanim's Shard/Scepter, secondary or situational spells, or accompanied by removed utility (e.g. no longer restores mana, no longer kills illusions, removed ability, cast-range or "affects only self" cuts) are net neutral or negative: don't predict "up" from them. A hero that loses a defining ability or mechanic, or has a mixed patch with a real nerf, leans "down" when it was popular or mid-pick.
- Predict "flat" when the changes are tiny and the hero is mid or low in popularity. Flat is rare (about 15% of cases), so with any substantive change lean to up or down. A hero with an all-talent patch that reshuffles many talents on a popular hero (>9%) leans "down" rather than "flat".
