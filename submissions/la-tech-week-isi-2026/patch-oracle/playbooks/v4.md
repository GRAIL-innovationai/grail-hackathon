- Nerfs (lower damage or stats, higher cooldowns or mana costs) to a hero's core abilities make pros pick it less: predict "down". Buffs to core abilities make pros pick it more: predict "up".
- Talent swaps, talent number tweaks, minor numeric tweaks, and quality-of-life or text changes are noise, so don't treat them as real buffs or nerfs. If a patch has only these, judge by prior popularity (see next rule) instead of defaulting to "flat". Very small changes on a mid or low-popularity hero (a couple of talent tweaks, a lifesteal-rule or text change, one minor number) are often truly "flat".
- **Popularity is the main tiebreaker for small or mixed changes, but top-third heroes (>10%) are not automatically "down".** Top-pick heroes with only talent swaps, removed facets, or trims to secondary abilities (slow duration, evasion, lifesteal-rule or creep-penalty changes) usually hold or rise, so predict "up" or hold. Predict "down" for them only when a core ability (main damage, stun, defining spell) is actually cut, or the hero loses a defining mechanic. Least-picked heroes (<=4.5%) rise after a clear buff to core abilities, and noise-only patches on them are "flat" (occasionally "up"). Middle-popularity heroes follow the net direction of the core ability changes.
- **The 7.36-style base attribute rework (large "Base Damage increased", lower "Damage gain per level", higher stat gains) is a mild buff, not a nerf.** If level-1 damage rises or stays the same and stat gains rise, predict "up" for mid and top heroes. For low-pick heroes predict "up" or "flat", not "down", even when level-30 damage falls by 45-60. A large level-30 drop only points "down" when level-1 damage is unchanged or lower and the hero is not top-pick. Switching to Universal with small stat or damage changes and no core ability buff is not a buff. Predict "flat" or "down" for low-pick heroes, especially if an ability is also nerfed.
- Removing a facet is not by itself a nerf. Treat it as neutral unless the hero's core ability was also cut.
- Ability numeric buffs only count if they hit the hero's main pick reason (primary damage, stun, core spell). Buffs limited to Aghanim's Shard/Scepter, secondary or situational spells, or accompanied by removed utility (no longer restores mana, removed ability, cast-range or "affects only self" cuts) are net neutral or negative: don't predict "up" from them. A hero that loses a defining ability or mechanic, or has a mixed patch with a real nerf to a core spell, leans "down" when it was mid-pick.
- Predict "flat" when the changes are tiny and the hero is mid or low in popularity. Flat is rare (about 15% of cases), so with any substantive change lean to up or down.
